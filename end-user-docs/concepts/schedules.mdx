# Schedules and reminders

Two tools cover time-based work. The `scheduler` tool creates jobs that run an
instruction as a full agent turn, once at a set time or on a recurring cadence.
The `reminder` tool sets personal reminders that simply notify you. Both are
available in chat and over [remote MCP](/docs/reference/remote-mcp) as `wf_scheduler`
and `wf_reminder`.

## Scheduled jobs

Ask the agent to schedule something, or call the tool directly. A job carries:

- **Timing:** `in_time` for a relative delay such as `2h`, `at` for an absolute ISO
  8601 time, or `every_secs` or `cron` for a recurring cadence, with an optional
  `timezone` (default UTC).
- **Instruction:** the `message` the agent receives when the job fires.
- **Delivery:** a Slack `channel_id` and optional `thread_id` for the result. Set
  `agent_owns_delivery` when the instruction tells the agent to post the result
  itself, so the scheduler does not post a duplicate.
- **Session:** every run starts in a fresh session by default, so no run carries
  another's transcript. `context = "conversation"` gives the job one session of its
  own to keep across runs: every run then replays all the runs before it, so each
  run costs more than the last. A recurring job asked to keep a session always asks
  for confirmation first. Where the result is delivered never decides the session.
- **Limits:** `max_runs` and `expires_in` switch a recurring job off automatically.
  Use them for anything that waits for a condition.

Actions: `create`, `list` (each job with its session, run count and spend so far),
`update`, `remove`, `run` (fire it now), `history` (recent runs and their cost), and
`set_bill_to` for administrators to charge a job to a system lane instead of the
owner's budget.

### Changing a job

`update` changes a job in place: its name, schedule, whether it is enabled, its
visibility, the tools its runs are offered, and its session. The owner of a job and
administrators can change it; nobody else can. The same edit is on the **Schedules**
page, where each job shows whether its session is **Fresh** or **Pinned**, how many
times it has run and what it has spent.

Switching a pinned job to fresh stops the growth at once: its next run starts in a
new session and none of its later runs can read the old one. Delivery, the owner,
and whose access and budget the job uses stay as they were. Pinning a job gives it
a new, empty session that belongs to that job alone, so it never joins a
conversation or another job's session.

A pinned session is also kept bounded. Once it holds as many messages as
`[compaction] chat_rollover_max_messages` (200 by default), the next run starts in
a successor session that carries a short dated tail of the last runs, not the
whole history.

A job created before sessions were minted per job may have been stored with the
name of the conversation it was created in, which other people's jobs could share.
Such a job keeps a session of that name apart for the person it runs as, so no one
else's runs reach it; switch it to fresh and pin it again to give it a new, empty
session of its own. Where its result is delivered does not change.

Changing a job asks for your confirmation, with the same cost estimate the tool
gives when creating one, when the change pins a recurring job or makes it run more
often at a projected cost over the confirmation threshold. The agent asks you in
the conversation; the **Schedules** page shows the estimate and a **Confirm and
save** button. A job's spend is shown to its owner and to administrators.

An administrator can stop an agent's recurring jobs keeping a session at all with
`pinned_sessions = false` under `[scheduler]`, in `workforce.toml` for every agent
or in one agent's own `config.toml`. A job that was pinned then runs fresh, and
its run history says so.

### Cost guardrails

Every fire of a recurring job is a full agent turn, so recurring jobs cost real
money. Recurring intervals have a configured minimum, and a job whose projected
daily cost exceeds the configured ceiling returns a `needs_confirmation` estimate.
The person has to approve it before the create is retried with `confirmed = true`.
Prefer, in order: an event-driven monitor over polling, the coarsest interval that
works, and an explicit `max_runs` or `expires_in`.

### Who a job runs as

A job runs as the identity that created it and uses that person's access to tools,
credentials, and data. A job is visible to its owner, or to everyone when saved as
global; administrators can see and manage every job.

A job you created runs like your own conversation with the agent: its prompt
knows your profile, notes, pins, preferences and memories, whatever tools it uses,
wherever it posts and whether you made it global. A job's prompt leaves that out
when someone else is in charge of it:

- an administrator created it to run as you;
- someone else last changed its schedule, its session, its tools or who may see
  it (an administrator's edit), until you open it and save it yourself; renaming,
  pausing or re-billing a job is not such a change;
- a heartbeat, monitor, workflow step or another job created it, or a sub-agent one
  of them started, until you save it yourself;
- the scheduler posts its reply into a shared channel, where it speaks to the room.

Charging a job to another budget changes nothing here.

## Reminders

Reminders are lighter: `set` with a message and a time, once or on a cadence,
`list`, and `cancel`. A reminder arrives as a direct message to you wherever it was
set. Use a reminder when you want a nudge, and a scheduled job when you want the
agent to do work.
